Active Research

ZingLab pursues focused, hypothesis-driven investigations into microbial ecology and its applications in animal health. Our current research portfolio centers on the oral microbiome of companion animals, with particular attention to the role of commensal bacterial strains in disease prevention and health maintenance.

Each project is designed with clearly defined objectives, reproducible methodology, and an eye toward translational application. We work with characterized reference strains sourced from qualified suppliers and handle all materials in accordance with applicable biosafety standards.

FELINE ORAL MICROBIOME PROJECT

Feline Oral Microbiome & Anticariogenic Probiotic Research

Investigating the application of Streptococcus oralis subsp. dentisani as a candidate probiotic agent for periodontal disease prevention in geriatric domestic cats.

Background:
Periodontal disease is the most prevalent chronic condition diagnosed in domestic cats, with prevalence estimates exceeding 70% in cats over three years of age. In geriatric populations, untreated oral disease is associated with systemic complications including bacteremia, renal inflammation, and compromised nutritional intake — all of which significantly reduce quality of life and longevity. Conventional management relies heavily on mechanical intervention and antimicrobial therapy. However, repeated antibiotic exposure carries risks of resistance development and microbiome disruption, underscoring the need for alternative, microbiome-compatible approaches.

Rationale: Streptococcus oralis subsp. dentisani is a naturally occurring commensal bacterium isolated from caries-free human tooth surfaces. It is recognized for its capacity to inhibit the growth of cariogenic and periodontopathic bacteria through competitive exclusion and the production of antimicrobial peptides. Its documented anticariogenic activity in the human oral microbiome provides a compelling basis for investigating analogous protective mechanisms in the feline oral environment.

Objectives:

  • Evaluate the in vitro inhibitory effects of S. oralis subsp. dentisani (Strain 7746) against key bacterial species implicated in feline periodontal disease, including Porphyromonas gulae, Tannerella forsythia, and Treponema spp.
  • Assess the stability and viability of S. oralis subsp. dentisani under conditions that mimic the feline oral environment, including variations in temperature, pH, and exposure to salivary enzymes.
  • Investigate the compatibility of S. oralis subsp. dentisani with the broader feline oral microbiome through co-culture experiments with representative commensal species.
  • Generate preliminary data to support the development of a probiotic formulation suitable for administration in companion animals, with an emphasis on delivery methods that optimize strain viability and colonization potential.

Current Status:
Strain acquisition in progress. In vitro characterization phase anticipated to commence upon receipt and quality verification of reference material.

Strain Reference:
Streptococcus oralis subsp. dentisani; Strain 7746 — sourced from Creative Biolabs Live Biotherapeutics Division (CAT#: LBST-097FG). Risk Group 1. Cultured on Columbia blood agar under microaerophilic conditions at 37°C.

Early Warning System for Dementia Symptoms

Simple website for early dementia symptom detection

A simple, at-home digital assessment to detect early signs of dementia.

Background:
Dementia is a progressive neurological disorder that affects memory, thinking, and behavior. Early detection is crucial for managing the condition and improving quality of life. This project aims to develop a simple, at-home digital assessment to identify early signs of dementia.

In collaboration with researchers at Loughborough University, ZingLab developed one of the most accessible early-onset dementia risk tools available today: a simple, at-home digital assessment that takes minutes to complete and requires no clinical setting, no personal data, and no prior medical history.

The assessment, based on Loughborough University research, is designed around validated cognitive indicators and asks only for a participant's age, then generates a meaningful risk categorization based on their response time. Users who opt in for follow-up can share an email address alongside a small set of optional demographic data points; everyone else participates fully anonymously. No personal data is sold, shared, or retained beyond what participants explicitly choose to provide.

The tool has demonstrated strong effectiveness in reaching across demographic groups, to provide a growing dataset of anonymous, researcher-accessible responses. This is already informing new lines of inquiry into early cognitive decline. We are all about taking rigorous science and putting it directly in the hands of the people who need it.
