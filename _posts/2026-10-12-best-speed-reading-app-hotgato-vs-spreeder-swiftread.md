Speed reading apps all promise the same thing: get through more text in less time. But once you look past the landing pages, they differ wildly on the things that actually matter — **what files they open, whether your documents get uploaded to someone's server, what's free versus paywalled, and how readable the output is at 500+ words per minute.**

We built [HotGato](https://hotgato.com/?ref=zinglab-blog) because we were frustrated with the options. So, yes, we're biased. But we also use the competition, and we think the comparison below is honest about where each one shines.

## The contenders

- **[HotGato](https://hotgato.com/?ref=zinglab-blog)** — free, open-source, in-browser RSVP reader with extensions for Chrome, Firefox and LibreOffice.
- **[Spreeder](https://www.spreeder.com/)** — one of the oldest web speed readers, with a free basic reader and paid training programs.
- **[Swiftread](https://swiftread.com/)** — a popular Chrome extension for speed reading web pages, with a paid Pro tier.
- **[Readsy](https://readsy.co/)** — a free web reader built on Spritz-style RSVP.
- **[Outread](https://outread.app/)** — a polished Apple-platform app built around guided highlighting.
- **[AccelaReader](https://accelareader.com/)** — a bare-bones, paste-and-go RSVP page.

## Why HotGato wins

### 1. Your documents never leave your machine

This is the big one. When you speed read a contract, a manuscript, a medical paper, or a pile of internal reports, you probably shouldn't be uploading it to a third party. HotGato does all of its work inside your browser — file parsing, settings, everything — and can run with no internet connection at all. There is nothing to sign up for and nothing stored on a server. That's why teams can get IT approval to use it on confidential material, and why we offer [business and enterprise licensing](https://hotgato.com/?ref=zinglab-blog) for organizations that want support.

### 2. It actually opens your files

Most speed readers are built around pasting text or reading the current web page. That's fine for blog posts. It's not fine for a 300-page EPUB or a PDF from your professor. HotGato reads **TXT, PDF, DOCX, EPUB, RTF, ODT, HTML and Markdown** out of the box, for free.

### 3. Readability at high speed

Raw RSVP — one word, fixed rhythm — falls apart at speed because real text isn't uniform. HotGato's adjustable **punctuation pause** gives sentences room to land, **chunking** lets you see two or three words at once when that reads more naturally, and it **automatically slows down for numbers and special characters** (so "3.89" and "3.98" don't blur together). These are small touches. Together they're the difference between "fast" and "fast and I remember what I read."

### 4. Fonts for real brains

HotGato includes a **Bionic** reading font, a **dyslexia-friendly** font, and standard sans-serif, serif, and monospace options, with adjustable size. No other app in this roundup gives you that range for free.

### 5. It's everywhere you read

Use the [web app](https://hotgato.com/?ref=zinglab-blog), the [Chrome extension](/projects/hotgato-extension) (select text, right-click, speed read), the Firefox add-on, or the LibreOffice integration. And because it's [open source on GitHub](https://github.com/TheRedJalapeno/HotGato_SpeedReader), developers can audit it, fork it, or extend it.

## Where the competition is genuinely good

- **Spreeder** is a strong choice if what you want is a **structured course** — guided lessons, exercises, and progress tracking — and you're happy paying for it.
- **Outread** is very polished on **iPhone, iPad, and Mac** if you prefer a native app and guided line highlighting over one-word-at-a-time RSVP.
- **Swiftread** is a good pick if you **only** speed read web articles in Chrome and want a mature extension.
- **AccelaReader** is fine for a 30-second test drive with pasted text.

If none of those describe you exactly, HotGato is the one to use.
