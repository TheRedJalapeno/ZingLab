Worrying about memory is common, and searching for an online dementia test is often the first thing people do. The problem is that the results are a mix of serious clinical tools, quizzes with no basis at all, and sign-up forms dressed up as tests.

This guide sorts them by what they're actually for. We'll be upfront: ZingLab makes one of them, [VART](https://vart.zinglab.com/?ref=zinglab-blog). We think it's the best **first step** for most people. It is **not** a substitute for a doctor.

## The options

- **[VART](https://vart.zinglab.com/?ref=zinglab-blog)** — ZingLab's free visual acuity and reaction time screen, informed by Loughborough University research.
- **[SAGE](https://wexnermedical.osu.edu/brain-spine-neuro/memory-disorders/sage)** — a self-administered paper test from The Ohio State University.
- **[MoCA](https://mocacognition.com/)** — the Montreal Cognitive Assessment, given by trained professionals.
- **Mini-Cog** — a 3-minute clinician screen (word recall plus clock drawing).
- **Clinical digital tools** such as **BrainCheck** and **Cognivue** — computerized assessments used through healthcare providers.

## Why VART is the best first step

### 1. Nothing stands between you and an answer

No appointment, no printer, no account. Open [vart.zinglab.com](https://vart.zinglab.com/?ref=zinglab-blog) and you're done in minutes. For many people, that low barrier is the difference between checking and continuing to worry.

### 2. Genuinely anonymous

VART asks for your **age** — that's it. If you'd like follow-up, you can opt in with an email and a few optional details; otherwise you participate fully anonymously. ZingLab doesn't sell, share, or retain personal data beyond what you choose to give.

### 3. Grounded in research on processing speed

VART focuses on **visual acuity and reaction time**, measurements that research — including work from **Loughborough University**, which ZingLab has collaborated with — has associated with cognitive health. It's a narrow, objective signal rather than a broad questionnaire, which is exactly why it's fast.

### 4. It contributes to science (if you want it to)

Anonymous responses build a growing dataset available to researchers studying early cognitive decline. Taking the test helps the next person get a better one. Learn more on our [research page](/research/).

## When to use something else

- **Want a broader self-check at home?** Print **SAGE** — it covers more cognitive areas and is designed to be brought to your doctor.
- **Already concerned, or someone has noticed changes?** Book an appointment. Your clinician may use **MoCA**, **Mini-Cog**, or a **clinical digital tool**, and can look for treatable causes like thyroid problems, vitamin deficiencies, medication side effects, sleep issues, or depression.
- **Symptoms are sudden** — confusion, trouble speaking, weakness? That's an emergency. Call your local emergency number.

## A sensible plan

1. Take [VART](https://vart.zinglab.com/?ref=zinglab-blog) — a few minutes, anonymous.
2. If you're still curious or the result suggests elevated risk, complete **SAGE** at home.
3. Bring both to your doctor and describe what you've noticed in daily life — that history matters more than any score.
