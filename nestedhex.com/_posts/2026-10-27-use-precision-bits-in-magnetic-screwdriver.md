You have a precision bit kit and a magnetic screwdriver you like. Here's how to use them together.

## What you need

- A standard magnetic screwdriver.
- The 4mm precision bit for your screw (see [which bit fits which device](/blog/precision-screwdriver-bit-types/)).
- A [Nested Hex adapter](/).

## Steps

1. **Seat the adapter.** Push the Nested Hex adapter into your screwdriver's bit socket, just like a normal bit.
2. **Drop in the precision bit.** Insert the 4mm bit into the adapter. The magnet holds it in place.
3. **Drive the screw.** Line the tip up square to the screw head, press in, and turn.
4. **Swap tips in seconds.** Pull the precision bit out and drop in the next one. The adapter stays in the screwdriver.

## Tips to avoid stripped screws

- **Match the tip exactly.** A Phillips 00 in a Phillips 000 screw will cam out. Test-fit before you turn: the bit should sit snug with no wobble.
- **Push more, twist less.** Small screws strip when the bit rides up out of the head. Firm downward pressure matters more than torque.
- **Keep it straight.** A full-size handle makes it easier to keep the driver square to the screw, which is half the reason to use one.
- **Go slow on the first turn.** Factory screws and threadlocker need a firm, steady start, not a fast spin.
- **Keep track of screws.** Small screws are often different lengths. Lay them out in order on a magnetic mat or a sheet of paper as they come out.

## Why bother?

Precision handles are fine for quick jobs. For tight screws, long sessions, or anything you'd hate to strip, a full-size handle is just easier to control, and the bits you already own keep working.
